Open the hopper of a grinder that has never been properly cleaned and smell the burr chamber. That smell — sour, oily, faintly like old nuts — is rancid coffee oil, and a proportion of every dose passes through it.

This is the flavour floor. No grind adjustment, no recipe change and no better coffee gets you below it, because every dose is being seasoned on its way out.

## What accumulates, and where

- **The burr chamber and burr carrier.** Oils and fines pack into the corners and behind the burr mounting.
- **The chute.** The worst offender. It is where ground coffee slows down, and where oil-bound fines build into a hard coating that narrows the passage.
- **The exit and forks.** Where the dose leaves. Buildup here changes how the dose falls and increases the mess.
- **The hopper and its walls.** A thin oil film from the beans, which goes rancid on its own schedule.
- **The gate and hopper seat**, which nobody looks at.

The chute is the one to attend to, because a partially coated chute both holds stale grounds and increases retention — so it makes the problem worse over time.

## Never put burrs in water

Steel burrs rust, and a rusted burr is scrap. Two rules with no exceptions:

- **No water, no dishwasher, no soaking.** Not for burrs, not for the burr carrier if it is steel.
- **Brush dry.** A stiff brush and compressed air or a vacuum. If a manufacturer-approved solvent is used, dry it immediately and completely.

The hopper is different — that is plastic or glass and washes in warm soapy water, but it must be dried completely before beans go back in. Any moisture in a hopper accelerates staling badly.

## Mark the setting before you strip anything

Removing burrs loses the grind reference on most grinders. Before disassembly:

- Note the current setting number, or mark the collar against the body with a pencil or a piece of tape.
- Accept that on stepless grinders you will be re-dialling afterwards regardless.
- Plan burr work for the end of the day, not fifteen minutes before opening.

## The routine

**Daily.** Wipe the hopper exterior and the area around the grinder — chaff gets everywhere and finds its way into the espresso machine's air intakes. Purge as per the retention routine.

**Weekly.** Take the hopper off. Empty the beans out. Brush the bean gate, the hopper seat and as much of the burr chamber as the design lets you reach without stripping. Vacuum out what you loosen rather than pushing it further in.

**Weekly or fortnightly, cleaning tablets.** Food-safe grinder cleaning granules — usually a cereal or rice-based product — run through as if they were coffee. They absorb oil and carry fines out. Follow with a purge of sacrificial coffee, since the first doses after tablets taste of tablets.

Check the manufacturer approves them first. A few grinders specifically prohibit them, and using them on those grinders is a warranty question.

**Monthly to quarterly, depending on volume.** Strip the burrs out. Brush the chamber, the carrier, both burr faces, the chute along its whole length, and the exit. This is where a proper chute brush earns its place. Reassemble, reset the setting, re-dial.

## Signs it is overdue

- A rancid or sour smell from the hopper or chamber.
- Visible dark oily residue on the burr carrier or chute walls.
- Grounds clumping more than they used to at the same setting.
- Retention creeping up — bigger purge needed to clear the previous coffee.
- The dose taking longer to deliver at the same setting, or falling unevenly.
- A dull, flat baseline in the cup across every coffee on the bar.

That last one is the expensive symptom, because it gets diagnosed as a roast problem. A bar can change coffee suppliers over a dirty grinder.

## Frequency by volume

| Daily throughput | Tablets / deep brush | Full burr strip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under 3 kg | Fortnightly | Quarterly |
| 3–8 kg | Weekly | Monthly to six-weekly |
| Over 8 kg | Twice weekly | Monthly |

Adjust for the coffee. Dark, oily roasts foul a grinder considerably faster than light ones, and a bar running dark roasts at volume can need the chute cleared weekly.

## Decaf and the shared-grinder problem

Decaf beans are often processed and stored differently and can behave stickier in a grinder. If the bar has a dedicated decaf grinder — and it should — it will typically have low throughput and high oil accumulation relative to its use.

Low-volume grinders need cleaning on a *calendar* schedule, not a throughput one. A decaf grinder doing 200 g a day still needs its monthly clean, and because it is barely used it is the one that gets forgotten for a year.

## Put it in the routine with the burr log

The weekly clean belongs on the cleaning rota with a named owner, like the drain and the backflush. The burr strip belongs in the maintenance log alongside the throughput figure, because the throughput number is what drives burr replacement — which is the next post.

Two lines in a logbook: date cleaned, kilograms through since the last one. That is enough to answer both the cleaning question and the burr-life question later.
