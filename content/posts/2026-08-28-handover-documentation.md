Most installations end with a verbal walkthrough, a manual left on a shelf, and a business card. Two years later nobody at the site knows what pressure the machine was set to, when the filter was last changed, or who installed it.

The handover file is twenty minutes of work at the end of a job. It is the difference between a machine with a history and a machine that has to be re-diagnosed from scratch every time something changes.

## The baseline is the part with value

Manuals can be downloaded. Serial numbers are on the machine. The thing that cannot be recovered later is **what the machine was doing when it was known to be right.**

- Supply voltage measured at the machine, under load.
- Static and flowing water pressure at the inlet.
- Source water and treated water test results, with dates.
- Filter model, cartridge model, install date, rated volume.
- Steam pressure and brew temperature settings, plus the measured group offset.
- Pump pressure under flow.
- Group flow rates, per group.
- Volumetric dose settings, per group.
- Grinder dose consistency: ten weighed doses and the spread.
- Ambient temperature behind the bar and at the grinder intake.
- Thermal recovery figures from the load test.

Every future diagnostic question is a comparison against that list. Without it, "has the pump always been at 8.5 bar?" is unanswerable, and you end up guessing at what normal looks like.

> A single reading proves the machine worked once. A series proves what is changing. The handover file is the first entry in the series, and it is the only one you cannot go back and take.

## The rest of the file

- **Manuals** for machine, grinders, filtration and any refrigeration.
- **Warranty registration confirmation**, with dates and terms, including the water quality conditions the warranty depends on.
- **Photographs**: data plates, the under-counter void with everything labelled, the filter head, the drain air gap, the electrical isolator, the panel schedule.
- **As-installed notes**: which circuit, which isolator, where the water shutoff is, where the drain runs.
- **Cleaning schedules** with quantities, laminated for the point of use and a copy in the file.
- **The escalation rules** — the three conditions that mean stop and call.
- **Service contacts**, with hours, and what counts as an emergency.
- **Consumables list**: part numbers for gaskets, screens, baskets, cartridges and burrs, so a reorder does not require a phone call.

## Assume the recipient leaves

This is the practical failure. You hand the file to the manager who commissioned the fit-out, and they move on within eighteen months. The file goes into a drawer, then into a skip during a refurbishment.

What survives:

- **A digital copy**, emailed to a role address rather than a person, and stored somewhere the business controls.
- **Your own copy.** You will be asked, and being the only party holding the record is a service in itself.
- **A label on the machine.** Serial number, install date, your contact details, and the filter cartridge model. Stuck somewhere permanent. When a new manager finds a machine with a phone number on it, they call the right person instead of the cheapest search result.
- **A tag at the filter head** with cartridge model, install date and rated volume, per the water install.

## The handover meeting

Do it with the people who will actually operate the machine, not only the owner, and do it with the machine hot.

Walk the daily routine physically rather than describing it. Show them the isolators for water and power and make them find them again. Do the first backflush together. Change the filter cartridge once, in place, so they know it is possible.

Then be explicit about the three escalation conditions — a tripping breaker, escaping steam or water, a gauge outside its normal band — and be equally explicit that everything else can wait for a scheduled visit. A team that knows the difference calls you at the right time.

## Book the follow-up before you leave

Two weeks. Thermal cycling settles fittings, the team's habits become visible, and the first genuine faults surface in the first fortnight.

Make it part of the installation rather than a separate sale. It is also the first opportunity to compare readings against the baseline, which is when the file starts earning its keep — and it demonstrates to the client that the numbers were recorded for a reason rather than as paperwork.

## Why it pays for you

Beyond the professional argument, the file is commercially useful in three ways. It supports warranty claims, because a manufacturer declining a scale-damage claim is answered with treated-water test records. It shortens every future visit, because you arrive knowing what the machine should be doing. And it makes you the obvious person to call, because you are holding the only complete record of the equipment.
