Barista training covers dose, grind, texture and latte art. Machine training covers keeping several kilowatts and a pressure vessel healthy, and it is usually about ten minutes of an induction.

The gap is where most avoidable damage happens. A team can be excellent at making coffee and have no idea that a tripping breaker is a fault report rather than an inconvenience.

## Teach the numbers, not the adjectives

Every routine instruction should be executable by someone in their second week with no judgement required.

- Not "purge the grinder" — **"discard the first 15 g"**.
- Not "backflush with cleaner" — **"¼ tsp, 6 × (5 s on / 10 s off), then 10 rinse cycles"**.
- Not "check the machine is up to temperature" — **"steam gauge between 1.0 and 1.3 bar, brew reads 93 °C"**.
- Not "clean the wand" — **"purge, wipe with the blue cloth, purge again — every single drink"**.

Laminated, at the point of use. Not in a folder in the office.

## What the gauges should read

Show every barista the normal band for each gauge, and write it on a label next to the gauge itself. Most staff have never been told what the needles mean, so a needle in the wrong place is invisible to them.

Once they know, they become your monitoring system. A barista who says "the steam gauge has been sitting higher than that line since Tuesday" has given you a diagnosis before it became a failure.

## The three escalation conditions

These need to be a bright line, not a judgement call, because a busy person under pressure will always default to carrying on.

1. **A breaker, RCD or RCBO that trips.**
2. **Steam or water escaping from anywhere other than a wand or a group.**
3. **A gauge reading outside its normal band**, or pressure rising when the machine is not calling for heat.

Isolate at the local disconnect and call. Do not reset, do not keep an eye on it, do not finish the rush first.

> Be equally clear that *everything else* can wait for a scheduled visit. A rule that makes staff feel every oddity is an emergency gets ignored within a month. Three specific conditions get remembered.

## The five things never to do

Stated as prohibitions, because these are the ones that cause real damage and they all feel reasonable in the moment.

1. **Never reset a tripping breaker repeatedly.** The trip is the machine reporting an element failing to earth. Resetting it destroys the evidence and eventually the element.
2. **Never adjust or tighten a safety valve** to stop it weeping. A weeping safety valve is either finished or the boiler is over-pressuring. Both need a technician.
3. **Never shorten the backflush rinse.** Residual detergent tastes appalling and sits against seals overnight. Ten cycles means ten.
4. **Never knock pucks into the drain.** However convenient the fitting looks, compacted grounds block waste pipes.
5. **Never put burrs in water.** Steel burrs rust, and a rusted burr is scrap.

I would add a sixth for anyone tempted: never lock the portafilter in harder to stop a leak. Increasing effort to lock in is a symptom to report.

## Train for turnover

Hospitality turnover means the person you trained will likely be gone within a year and will have trained their replacement themselves, badly, by demonstration.

What survives turnover:

- **Written routines with quantities**, laminated at the point of use.
- **A named owner per task** on the cleaning rota, initialled and reviewed by someone senior. A checklist nobody reads stops being filled in honestly within a month.
- **One person designated as equipment lead**, who you deal with directly and who trains others. When they leave, retrain deliberately rather than hoping.
- **A short induction sheet** that a new starter physically signs, covering the escalation conditions and the five prohibitions.

## Verify by watching, not by asking

Everyone answers yes when asked whether they backflush properly. The routine is only real if you have seen it.

At the two-week follow-up, watch a close-down. Count the rinse cycles. Watch whether the wand gets purged after the jug comes away. Watch how the grinder gets purged at open. Watch how hard someone has to work to lock in a portafilter.

That observation tells you more about the machine's next five years than any measurement you take. It is also where you find out which written instruction was ambiguous — usually because the person is doing something reasonable that the sheet did not quite say.

## Explain why, once

Staff follow routines they understand and quietly abandon ones they do not.

It takes two minutes to explain that backflushing protects a valve that costs more than a case of detergent, that the rinse matters because the cleaner is caustic, that purging the wand stops milk being drawn back inside, and that the filter cartridge is what stands between the boiler and a scale bill.

People who know what a task prevents keep doing it when they are busy. People following an unexplained list stop as soon as the shift gets hard.
